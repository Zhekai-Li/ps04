# Replace one-shot drafting with a verified research pipeline

[Visual: A polished paragraph appears instantly, then dissolves into a pipeline of sources, tests, revisions, and approvals.]

The wrong unit of AI assistance is the generated paragraph. A fluent paragraph can conceal a false citation, a broken computation, or a dependency nobody checked. The more defensible unit is a verified workflow: explicit inputs, tools, tests, provenance, and revision.

Evidence from algorithm design shows why structure matters. The LACE framework gives a language model a verified problem contract, tool library, repair operations, and an iterative process for generating and selecting heuristics. On its combinatorial-optimization benchmark, LACE achieved a reported Valid Rate of 0.944; none of the compared LLM-based baselines exceeded 0.611. Valid Rate measured whether an algorithm produced feasible solutions for every test instance of a problem. [claim:02-c1]

[Visual: Labeled comparison—LACE Valid Rate 0.944; other LLM baselines no higher than 0.611. Caption: “CO-Bench feasibility metric.”]

That is not a controlled study of academic prose. But it demonstrates the advantage of surrounding generation with constraints, executable tools, evaluation, selection, and repair. For writing, the corresponding pipeline begins before drafting.

First, define the claim. Record what kind of support it needs: a primary study, a theorem, a dataset, or a computation. Second, let AI search, classify, compare, or propose wording. Third, verify the underlying source or run the test. Fourth, record the dependency. Only then should the claim enter the manuscript.

[Visual: Five gates: Claim → Required evidence → AI assistance → Verification → Manuscript.]

The cost of ignoring dependencies appears in a reported mathematics release. According to Retraction Watch, OpenAI withdrew three AI-produced manuscripts after a sign error invalidated an argument in one paper and constructions used by two dependent papers. The company also reported revisions to 14 other manuscripts, including proof repairs, corrected statements, clearer hypotheses and dependencies, and one obsolete-citation correction. [claim:02-c2]

This is one unusually large release, not a base-rate estimate for AI-assisted scholarship. Retraction Watch also reported OpenAI’s statement that roughly half of the results had been released unconfirmed. The episode nevertheless shows why verification cannot be postponed until after hundreds of polished outputs exist.

Citation checking needs the same discipline. In a SciSpace webinar, a journal and grant reviewer advises checking every AI-supplied DOI to ensure it resolves to the work it claims to identify. [claim:02-c3]

[Visual: A DOI is opened. Title, authors, venue, year, and cited proposition are checked one by one.]

Resolving the link is only the first test. The researcher must still confirm that the paper exists, the metadata match, and the source actually supports the sentence. SciSpace sells research software, so this is practitioner guidance rather than independent validation of its products. The procedure itself is straightforward and auditable.

Provenance can extend beyond citations. In a Latent Space interview, representatives of Periodic Labs say their system records conversations, intuitions, laboratory actions, computations, and code. Their stated goal is to train on the process of science rather than only its final output. [claim:02-c4]

[Visual: A laboratory timeline connects discussion notes, code commits, computation records, failed trials, and final results.]

Periodic Labs is describing a commercial platform, not presenting a controlled comparison. Still, the lineage principle transfers: keep the failed query, changed assumption, source excerpt, script version, and reason for accepting or rejecting a result. Privacy, security, confidential review, and proprietary-data rules may limit what can be logged, so provenance must be designed around those obligations.

Across these cases, the evidence supports an inference, not a universal guarantee: efficiency and rigor are more compatible when tests and provenance are embedded in every iteration. A polished output alone is not a quality-control signal. [claim:02-c5]

Use AI where it reduces search and revision costs. But make every externally checkable claim point to a source, every computational result point to an executable test, and every important dependency visible before submission.
